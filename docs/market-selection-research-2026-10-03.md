# Market-selection research — 2026-10-03

## Decision status

**Beauty/Wellness is no longer a selected launch market. It is one candidate in a market-selection gate.**

This research deliberately excludes DCL client history from market selection. Agency experience may inform implementation after the market is chosen, but it is not evidence of category attractiveness.

## Current platform facts

- Shopify Theme Store: 1,299 themes; 1,275 paid, 24 free (2026-10-03 snapshot).
- Shopify labels Clothing, Beauty, Jewelry & accessories, and Home as its top industries.
- Catalog filters: One Product 27; Few (2–10) 98; Some (11–100+) 861; Lots (500+) 313.
- Store Leads' 2026 Shopify dataset reports Apparel 27.0% of stores, Home & Garden 12.3%, Beauty & Fitness 11.0%, Food & Drink 214,730 stores, Health 143,439, Sports 116,279, Arts & Entertainment 106,580, Pets & Animals 77,029, Autos & Vehicles 73,204, Consumer Electronics 54,980.
- A July 2026 new-store dataset from FisherLeads reports Apparel 34%, Beauty 9.4%, Jewelry 9.2%, Food & beverage 7.5%, Home decor 7.4%, Outdoor & sports 6% among the classified new sites in that report.

Third-party store counts are directional because category taxonomies and detection methods differ from Shopify's Theme Store taxonomy.

## Candidate screen

### Tier A — deep-dive now

1. **Clothing**
   - strongest merchant-demand signal by a wide margin;
   - Shopify top-industry designation;
   - very crowded theme supply: independent September 2026 count reports 261 Theme Store clothing themes;
   - demanding merchandising: variants, swatches, size/fit, collection discovery, lookbooks, quick purchase, large catalogs;
   - opportunity only if we find a structural/experience gap that incumbents do not already solve well.
   - Verdict: huge demand, huge competition. Must not select merely because TAM is largest.

2. **Beauty / Wellness**
   - Beauty is a Shopify top industry; Beauty & Fitness is #3 in Store Leads' current Shopify category data;
   - strong new-store formation signal;
   - strong premium visual/PDP storytelling fit;
   - heavily served by Prestige and many generalist/specialist themes;
   - wellness may broaden health/subscription/education use cases but increases compliance/content complexity.
   - Verdict: attractive demand, but prior selection was premature. Remains candidate, not default.

3. **Home**
   - Shopify top industry and #2 category in Store Leads;
   - visual merchandising matters; catalogs can range from focused brands to large furniture/decor assortments;
   - product dimensions/materials, room/context imagery, bundles/sets, swatches and delivery information create meaningful UX needs;
   - strong existing theme supply plus Shopify's own free Horizon-family options.
   - Verdict: large market with credible premium-design demand; deserves equal deep-dive to Beauty.

4. **Jewelry & accessories**
   - Shopify top-industry designation and strong new-store signal (Jewelry 9.2% in July FisherLeads classified launches);
   - high visual/premium-brand fit and considered-purchase trust needs;
   - independent September 2026 research reports 116 Theme Store themes in Jewelry & Accessories—materially less crowded than Clothing;
   - close-up media, variants/materials, gifting, sizing, craftsmanship, certification/trust and luxury storytelling create focused surface opportunities.
   - Verdict: particularly interesting demand/competition balance; must be deep-dived.

5. **Food & drink**
   - large Shopify store population and meaningful new-store formation;
   - repeat purchase, subscriptions, bundles, ingredients/nutrition, flavors, pack sizes, gifting and local/in-person commerce can create differentiated needs;
   - current Theme Store results show specialist competition (Local/Caffeine, Lively, Airwave, Fizz, etc.);
   - perishable/shipping/age-related subsegments create support complexity.
   - Verdict: credible candidate; needs segmentation so “food” does not become too broad.

### Tier B — investigate if Tier A fails to produce a defendable opportunity

- Sports: large store base, but spans apparel, equipment and hobby niches with divergent needs.
- Pets: meaningful store base and repeat-purchase potential, but smaller demand signal.
- Electronics: higher catalog/spec/comparison complexity and support burden; smaller store count than Tier A.
- Auto: fitment and compatibility needs can exceed what a theme alone should own.
- Kids/Toys: real market but weaker top-level demand signal and broad product behavior.
- Art: strong visual storytelling but lower monetization confidence for a premium theme.

### Tier C — do not lead v1 without contrary evidence

Bags, Entertainment, Garden, Hardware, Office, Services and Shoes as standalone launch categories. Several can be served as adjacent presets later, but current evidence does not justify prioritizing them ahead of Tier A.

## What this research changes

The previous Beauty/Wellness-first declaration is superseded.

The next decision must compare Tier A candidates on:
1. merchant demand/current store base;
2. new-store formation;
3. Theme Store supply and incumbent strength;
4. premium price acceptance;
5. recurring review complaints/unmet needs;
6. architecture-level differentiation potential under Shopify originality rules;
7. catalog and merchandising complexity;
8. ability for a theme (not an app) to solve the problem;
9. support/QA burden;
10. demo-store and merchandising quality required to compete.

No market will be selected from TAM alone. No market will be selected from DCL history.

## Current signal

The evidence does **not** support declaring Beauty/Wellness the winner.

The strongest deep-dive set is **Clothing, Beauty/Wellness, Home, Jewelry & Accessories, and Food & Drink**.

Among those, **Jewelry & Accessories is the most interesting challenger at this stage** because Shopify itself identifies it as a top industry, new-store formation is substantial, premium visual presentation matters, and the available 2026 category-count evidence suggests materially lower Theme Store crowding than Clothing. This is a hypothesis for the next evidence round, not the final market decision.

## Next evidence round

For each Tier A market:
- capture current Theme Store category supply from a reproducible source;
- sample the leading and newest specialist themes;
- collect price/review/preset data;
- mine recent negative/neutral reviews for category-relevant complaints;
- identify features that are table stakes vs genuine surface-system gaps;
- inspect demos and, where legitimate, editor depth;
- map support/QA burden;
- produce a commercial opportunity matrix with evidence confidence.

Only then select the launch market and resume market-specific M1 visual work.
