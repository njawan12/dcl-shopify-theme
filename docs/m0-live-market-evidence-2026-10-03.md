## What this invalidates

The market evidence rejects several weak differentiation claims:

1. **More sections/features** is not differentiation. Competitors already advertise 30–40+ sections and dense commerce/marketing feature sets.
2. **Premium visual design** is not differentiation. Prestige, Impact, Motion, Pipeline and others explicitly compete on premium/luxury/visual presentation.
3. **Mobile-first** is not differentiation by itself. Motion and Broadcast explicitly sell mobile-first/mobile optimization.
4. **No-code/customizable** is not differentiation by itself. Symmetry sells drag-and-drop customization; Broadcast reviews include merchants describing code-free changes; multiple themes are praised for ease of customization.
5. **Built-in conversion features** is not differentiation. Enterprise, Impact, Broadcast, Impulse and Pipeline already compete heavily here.

## Review evidence: recurring signals

Public reviews are not prevalence estimates and cannot replace interviews. They are useful as problem signals.

Observed signals across the sample include:

- Strong support is repeatedly a major part of perceived theme value, even for mature themes.
- Some merchants still need substantial customization or custom code for ordinary desired outcomes.
- Update regressions and custom-code compatibility create merchant anxiety/work.
- Mobile image/text adaptation and cropping remain failure modes.
- Large feature sets do not guarantee the right feature mix for a merchant.
- Merchants praise ease of use when it is present, so operability is valuable but is not unoccupied territory.
- App/integration issues can pull theme support into troubleshooting outside pure theme code.
- Internationalization controls such as visible language/currency placement can materially affect merchant satisfaction.

Representative dated examples recorded during the refresh:

- Impulse, Apr 20 2026: merchant reported that the theme required substantial customization to become usable for them, while praising support.
- Impulse/Dune, Mar 10 2026: merchant reported mobile image/text transition problems and slow support resolution.
- Impulse/Apothecary, Aug–Sep 2026: non-technical merchants explicitly described needing coder/support assistance; another merchant described header-design limitations.
- Symmetry, Jan 15 2026: merchant described the theme as not user-friendly despite its customization proposition; Nov 6 2025 review described basic product-list display needs still requiring custom work.
- Motion, Sep 2 2025: merchant described extensive custom creation needs and image-cropping problems.
- Prestige, Mar 17 2025: merchant reported basic customization repeatedly leading to custom-code recommendations.
- Impact, Dec 2 2025: merchant highlighted uncertainty about whether theme updates affect custom code.
- Enterprise, Nov 27 2025: merchant said the theme included unwanted features while missing wanted ones; other reviews describe support/custom coding and mobile/performance concerns.
- Pipeline, Jan/Jun 2026: merchants requested guidance before extensive changes and more visible language/currency selectors.
- Broadcast reviews demonstrate the counter-evidence: merchants often praise ease of customization and even code-free changes. This means “developer independence” alone is not a defensible whitespace claim.

## Refined opportunity hypothesis

The evidence supports testing a narrower proposition:

> **Job-oriented composition with bounded decisions:** help a marketing/ecommerce operator reach a coherent commercial outcome (Product Launch, Paid Landing Page, Collection Launch, Editorial Story, Product Education) with fewer setup decisions and less theme expertise than a section-catalog-first workflow, while retaining premium output and Shopify-native editing.

This remains a **hypothesis**, not a validated gap. The public evidence only establishes that competitors are feature-rich and that customization/support friction still exists for some merchants.

## Candidate workflow gaps to test — not yet accepted as proven

These are the specific gaps the task-based teardown and merchant interviews must now attempt to falsify:

1. **Blank-canvas/section-catalog decision load:** Can competitors start a Product Launch or Paid Landing job with a coherent composition, or must merchants select/sequence many generic sections?
2. **Cross-surface continuity:** Can a merchant carry one launch argument coherently from campaign → collection → PDP without rebuilding/repeating content or relying on custom implementation?
3. **Structured-but-optional product education:** Can standard product data look complete while optional benefits/steps/evidence enhance the PDP without making structured setup mandatory?
4. **Mobile recovery without custom intervention:** Can merchants resolve long titles, media crop/priority, missing media/reference, and sticky collisions through bounded native controls?
5. **Customization without complexity displacement:** Do large section/block catalogs actually reduce work, or move the burden into choosing among many settings/sections and support requests?

M0 requires at least three of these to survive live competitor task teardown and merchant evidence.

## Remaining M0 blockers after this refresh

- Complete and code at least 50 recent reviews across the competitor sample with date, topic and sentiment.
- Perform task-based live-demo teardowns on phone and desktop for the five candidate workflow gaps.
- Conduct at least 8 target-merchant interviews (8–12 planned), majority Beauty & Wellness and at least three across adjacent candidate verticals.
- Confirm concept comprehension and repeated launch/PDP-maintenance pain strongly enough to justify M1.
- Record founder ownership/support/design/QA/demo-investment decisions.
- Hold the explicit M0 go/no-go review.

Until those are complete, M0 remains open and M2 remains blocked.
