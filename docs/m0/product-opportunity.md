# M0 Product Opportunity

**Status:** revised product-direction baseline, 2 October 2026

**Decision:** proceed to M1 discovery and architecture validation; do not begin production theme implementation.

## What changed and why

The available repository history contains no earlier M0 documents to edit or quote. This document therefore records the requested correction as the single baseline rather than pretending that an unavailable draft was preserved. When prior research is recovered, useful evidence should be merged into this baseline, not maintained as a competing strategy.

The direction changes from a beauty-only product to a premium, multi-vertical DTC theme. Beauty/wellness remains the first preset and demonstration vertical because it provides a demanding test of education, proof, routines, ingredients and visual polish. It is a proving ground, not an architectural boundary. The differentiation hypothesis also changes from feature breadth to merchant operability.

## Revised target merchant

The primary buyer is a marketing or ecommerce manager at a design-conscious DTC brand who:

- owns launches, campaigns, merchandising and routine content changes;
- values an agency-quality visual system but cannot rely on a developer for ordinary work;
- has a focused catalogue or curated collections, rather than marketplace-scale complexity;
- needs strong mobile conversion and sophisticated product explanation;
- will accept guardrails when they make outcomes faster and more consistent; and
- may use metafields and metaobjects, but should not need them for a credible first launch.

The theme should serve founder-led brands and small internal creative teams secondarily. It is not primarily for merchants who want pixel-level freedom, a general-purpose page builder, heavily customized B2B workflows or maximum feature count.

## Positioning

> A premium Shopify theme for design-conscious DTC brands that turns common commercial intents into polished, coherent storefront compositions—so marketing teams can launch and merchandise confidently without routine developer dependence.

This is **multi-vertical, not universal**. The durable centre is a shared DTC selling grammar: reveal a proposition, explain a product, establish proof, enable a decision and present a clear action. Vertical presets adapt art direction, content emphasis, product attributes and merchandising patterns without fragmenting the core.

## Product promise

1. Exceptional results before extensive configuration.
2. Intent-led starting points instead of a blank canvas.
3. Semantic, plain-language controls with restrained choices.
4. Mobile-first commerce and accessible interaction patterns.
5. Product storytelling connected to the buying decision.
6. Optional structured content that scales without becoming mandatory setup.
7. Strong merchandising across PDPs, collections and campaigns.

## First preset

**Beauty & Wellness — Ritual Launch** is the first preset and reference implementation. It should demonstrate:

- sensorial art direction without sacrificing legibility or speed;
- benefit, ingredient, usage, routine and evidence storytelling;
- variant and subscription-ready purchase presentation where native/platform capabilities permit;
- complementary routines and collection merchandising; and
- credible defaults when structured product data is absent.

The preset must avoid encoding beauty nouns into reusable primitives. For example, the core concept is `key attributes`, while the preset may label it “Ingredients”; `usage sequence` may render as “Routine”. Apparel could later label the same concepts “Materials” and “Complete the look”.

## Success hypothesis and falsification

The product succeeds if representative managers can start from an intent, replace content, tune a small number of brand-level decisions, and publish a coherent mobile page without documentation or code. M1 should test time-to-first-credible-page, setting comprehension, recovery from mistakes and consistency across sparse and rich catalogues.

The hypothesis is weakened if presets are merely demo-data bundles, if merchants must understand the internal narrative model, if cross-vertical support produces vague sections, or if Shopify's native template workflow makes intent selection too obscure. Those are M1 validation questions, not implementation details to defer.

## Serious objection

“Premium multi-vertical DTC” is commercially attractive but dangerously easy to make generic. Beauty, apparel and food differ in variant density, regulatory/evidence needs, replenishment, sizing, shelf life and merchandising. A shared visual skin is not enough. The direction is defensible only if M1 proves that a small set of semantic primitives can produce materially different, excellent workflows without multiplying settings. If that proof fails, the stronger product is a narrower beauty/wellness theme—not a compromised universal theme.
