# M0 Originality Strategy

## Originality thesis

Originality will come from the interaction of **architecture, merchant workflow and storefront decision flow**, not a novel coat of paint or a longer section list.

The product's signature is an intent-led composition system built entirely from native Shopify primitives. A merchant selects a commercial job, receives a deliberately paced template, and edits semantic content roles within guardrails. The storefront expresses a consistent decision narrative while allowing each preset to have a specific art direction and vocabulary.

## Three layers of meaningful differentiation

### 1. Architecture: compositions, contracts and graceful depth

- A small set of reusable narrative roles connects page templates rather than an undirected library of interchangeable sections.
- JSON templates encode proven starting compositions for intents.
- Sections define clear content contracts and bounded variants; they are not mini page builders.
- Dynamic sources can enrich repeated facts, comparisons and evidence, while inline blocks remain useful by default.
- Content gracefully falls back when optional metafields or metaobjects are absent.

### 2. Merchant workflow: choose intent, then adapt

- Template names use merchant jobs such as “Product Launch”, not internal system terminology.
- The initial hierarchy, rhythm, mobile order and calls to action are already resolved.
- Controls describe outcomes—emphasis, density, proof style, media behaviour—not implementation details or arbitrary pixel values.
- Advanced fields appear only where they unlock a concrete job.
- Preset-specific defaults create a credible first result without making preset markup a separate product fork.

### 3. Storefront experience: decision-aware continuity

- PDP, collection and campaign compositions share a recognisable progression without being identical funnels.
- Mobile layouts preserve decision order rather than merely stacking desktop modules.
- Product cards, comparison cues, proof and purchase actions use consistent content priorities.
- Narrative pacing and merchandising cooperate: editorial modules lead naturally into discovery and buying, rather than sitting above a generic grid.

## Why this is not “another premium theme”

An incumbent can also offer templates, presets and sections. Therefore no single item above is sufficient. Differentiation exists only if the combined workflow is demonstrably better:

1. a merchant starts with a named commercial intent;
2. the composition encodes an opinionated decision sequence;
3. sections expose role-specific, low-ambiguity controls;
4. the same structured product facts can appear coherently across contexts;
5. the experience remains complete without structured setup; and
6. a preset changes vocabulary, defaults and art direction without duplicating the architecture.

M1 must produce workflow maps and low-fidelity prototypes that make this combination inspectable. If it reduces to alternative JSON ordering plus styling, it is not original enough and development should pause.

## Originality review gates

At each milestone, maintain a provenance table for every major pattern: merchant problem, source observations, our design response, closest known analogue and substantive difference. Review the whole workflow against Prestige, Impulse, Symmetry, Broadcast and Impact. Remove features whose only rationale is parity unless they are baseline buying requirements.

## Avoiding imitation

- Do not reproduce a competitor demo's sequence, naming, signature transitions or distinctive layout.
- Do not use competitor screenshots as implementation specifications.
- Do not treat new animation or decorative variants as product originality.
- Do not advertise generic capabilities—mega menus, swatches, hotspots or quick buy—as the core innovation.
- Do not let the Beauty & Wellness preset hard-code a competitor's editorial aesthetic into the system.

## Proof required before Theme Store submission

- comparative task recordings showing lower composition and configuration burden;
- an architecture map demonstrating native Shopify implementation;
- visual and interaction comparison across the named competitive set;
- evidence that at least two vertical vocabularies fit without generic labels or architecture forks; and
- accessibility, performance and mobile-order audits of each intent composition.
