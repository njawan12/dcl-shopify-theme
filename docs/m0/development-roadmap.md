# M0 Development Roadmap

## Direction correction

M0 now ends with a multi-vertical DTC product thesis, Beauty & Wellness as the first preset, merchant operability as the differentiator, and intent-led native compositions as the main workflow. M1 is a validation and specification milestone. It is explicitly not production theme implementation.

## Exact recommended M1 scope

### 1. Evidence and competitive workflow audit

- Recover and normalize prior research into an evidence ledger.
- Audit the named premium themes by five tasks, including clicks/decisions, terminology, starting states, mobile defaults and required expertise.
- Record Theme Store originality and technical constraints from current primary sources.

**Exit:** evidence-backed opportunity statements and a provenance/difference table.

### 2. Merchant and content model

- Define primary/secondary personas and catalogue boundaries.
- Gather representative Beauty & Wellness content sets: sparse, typical and structured-rich.
- Map durable content roles and explicitly isolate regulated or vertical-specific concepts.

**Exit:** approved jobs, data inventory and non-goals.

### 3. Intent workflow prototypes

- Create editor-flow wireframes for all five intents.
- Create storefront wireframes for Product Launch and Collection Launch at mobile and desktop widths.
- Test how merchants select, duplicate, assign and safely modify native templates.

**Exit:** a manager can identify an intent and produce a coherent draft without internal terminology or a blank canvas.

### 4. Launch Narrative specification

- Define each role's purpose, required/optional inputs, valid neighbours, mobile priority and graceful omission.
- Map role sequences for PDP, collection and campaign/editorial contexts.
- Identify where proof and comparison require structured data versus local blocks.

**Exit:** the narrative changes concrete template and section decisions rather than remaining copywriting language.

### 5. Architecture decision records and technical spikes

- Specify native JSON templates, section groups, section/block boundaries, global tokens and preset layering.
- Spike dynamic sources and fallback behaviour using disposable prototypes only.
- Investigate landing-page navigation constraints, app blocks, localization, accessibility and performance budgets.

**Exit:** reviewed ADRs, risk results and no committed production architecture hidden in a prototype.

### 6. Beauty & Wellness preset brief

- Define art direction, typography/colour/token values, photography requirements and voice.
- Specify merchant-facing labels for benefits, ingredients/attributes, evidence, usage/routine and related products.
- Demonstrate credible sparse-data and rich-data outcomes.

**Exit:** a preset brief distinct enough to direct M2 visual design without hard-coding the core.

### 7. Usability and originality gates

- Run task tests with 5–8 target merchants or closest qualified proxies.
- Compare result quality, time, errors, assistance and confidence with a representative incumbent workflow.
- Conduct an originality review and a cross-vertical stress test using one apparel and one food/lifestyle content set.

**Exit:** evidence supports continuing, narrowing to beauty/wellness, or revising the workflow.

## M1 deliverables

1. Research evidence ledger and competitor workflow audit.
2. Product requirements and explicit non-goals.
3. Five editor-flow wireframes; two responsive storefront prototypes.
4. Launch Narrative composition specification.
5. Information architecture, schema sketches and ADR set.
6. Beauty & Wellness preset creative brief.
7. Usability report, originality assessment and M2 go/no-go recommendation.

## Not in M1

No production Liquid sections, final CSS/JavaScript system, complete theme scaffold, app integrations, Theme Store submission package, additional preset implementation or exhaustive setting schema. Disposable technical spikes must be clearly separated from production paths.

## Go/no-go criteria

Proceed to M2 only if users understand intent selection, the two deep prototypes outperform blank-canvas assembly, optional structured content falls back well, cross-vertical tests do not make primitives generic, and the originality review finds a defensible workflow-level difference. Narrow the product if cross-vertical abstraction weakens the first preset. Stop if differentiation depends primarily on styling or claims that competitors can trivially match with demo templates.
