# M0 Merchant Usability Principles

## North star

**Maximum useful flexibility; minimum cognitive complexity.** Ease of use means that common commercial work is obvious, safe and fast. It does not mean unlimited customization.

## Principles

1. **Start with intent, not inventory.** Offer a polished template for a job before exposing a catalogue of sections.
2. **Make the default publishable.** Defaults must establish hierarchy, spacing, mobile order and accessible contrast with realistic content.
3. **Use merchant language.** Name templates and controls by outcomes. “Evidence style” is better than “card mode 3”; internal Launch Narrative labels need not be visible.
4. **Prefer semantic choices.** Use choices such as quiet/balanced/bold, compact/comfortable and editorial/shoppable only when their consequences are predictable.
5. **Progressively disclose depth.** Put essential content and a few high-impact choices first. Advanced structured sources and exceptional overrides come later.
6. **Bound local freedom.** Typography, colour and spacing derive from global tokens; local overrides are rare and purposeful.
7. **Prevent invalid combinations.** If a configuration produces poor hierarchy, contrast, empty space or mobile order, do not offer it.
8. **Keep editing contextual.** Related content, proof and actions live together. Avoid requiring merchants to coordinate distant settings.
9. **Make optional data truly optional.** Structured content improves scale and consistency but cannot be the admission price for a finished page.
10. **Design mobile composition explicitly.** Mobile priority, crop, disclosure and action persistence are product decisions, not automatic stacking.
11. **Support safe evolution.** Merchants can duplicate a template, change intent or remove a module without silently losing content.
12. **Document exceptions, not basics.** A first-time user should succeed without reading a manual.

## Setting budget

M1 should establish budgets through testing rather than an arbitrary universal number. Each setting must pass three tests:

- it supports a frequent or high-value merchant decision;
- its effect is understandable from its label and preview; and
- it cannot be handled better by a global default, preset or automatic rule.

Settings that fail should be removed, not hidden in an “advanced” drawer. Progressive disclosure is not a landfill.

## Preset-led workflow

1. The merchant identifies the page job.
2. They create or assign the corresponding native JSON template.
3. The editor opens with an ordered, populated composition and concise guidance.
4. They replace primary content and connect products/collections.
5. They optionally bind reusable facts or evidence to dynamic sources.
6. They preview mobile and publish; advanced visual changes are exceptional.

No proprietary canvas, saved-layout service or non-native content database is introduced. Theme presets, JSON templates, section presets, blocks, template assignment, metafields and metaobjects do the work.

## Usability acceptance criteria for M1 prototypes

- A new participant can select the correct intent without learning internal terminology.
- They can explain what each top-level control will change before using it.
- They can produce a credible sparse-data page and enrich it with structured data.
- They can identify mobile content order and primary action.
- They can recover from removing or reordering a module.
- Tests record time, wrong turns, assistance, confidence and result quality—not satisfaction alone.
