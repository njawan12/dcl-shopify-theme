# M0 Market Research Synthesis

## Evidence boundary

This revision incorporates the live-market conclusions supplied by the product owner. The underlying URLs, captures, dates, prices and merchant interviews are not present in this repository, so competitor details below are hypotheses to verify rather than fresh primary research. M1 must attach an evidence ledger before making claims in a Theme Store submission or sales copy.

## What changed and why

Earlier product direction is described as beauty-led. The corrected interpretation is that beauty/wellness is an excellent first demonstration vertical, while the opportunity is premium DTC operability across a deliberately limited family of design-conscious brands. The competitive question is no longer “How can we ship more sections?” but “Can a manager reach and maintain a better outcome with less interpretation and less developer help?”

## Competitive frame

Prestige, Impulse, Symmetry, Broadcast and Impact establish a high bar for visual quality, merchandising and broad capabilities. Matching their checklists would neither prove originality nor create a durable reason to switch. Feature parity is a constraint at most, not the concept.

Three recurring opportunity hypotheses require validation:

1. **Blank-canvas cost:** a large section library transfers composition work to the merchant.
2. **Configuration tax:** many granular settings increase evaluation time, inconsistency and fear of breaking the design.
3. **Demo-to-reality gap:** a polished demo may depend on ideal assets, extensive setup or expert composition that ordinary teams cannot reproduce.

The proposed response is a system of native templates, section groups, presets and structured content contracts organized around merchant intent and decision flow.

## Jobs to be done

| Job | Current friction hypothesis | Product response |
|---|---|---|
| Launch a hero product | Assemble and order unrelated sections | Product Launch composition with guided content roles |
| Turn paid traffic into action | Remove navigation noise and improvise a landing page | Paid Landing Page template with focused progression |
| Introduce a collection | Balance campaign story and product discovery | Collection Launch composition with editorial merchandising |
| Publish a brand story | Avoid a blog-like wall of content | Editorial Story composition with deliberate pacing |
| Explain a complex product | Repeat facts manually across products | Product Education composition with optional structured sources |
| Maintain visual coherence | Interpret dozens of style controls per section | Global tokens, semantic variants and bounded local overrides |

## Vertical fit

The common architecture should support:

- **beauty/wellness:** benefits, ingredients, usage, routines and proof;
- **fashion/apparel:** materials, fit, styling, variants and coordinated looks;
- **lifestyle:** context of use, craft, specifications and related objects; and
- **food/beverage:** flavour, origin, nutrition/attributes, serving and bundles.

These are future preset candidates, not M1 deliverables. Each must prove a distinct content vocabulary and commerce flow before it is announced.

## Research required before implementation commitment

1. Conduct task-based tests with 5–8 target merchants using low-fidelity editor models.
2. Audit named competitors by workflow: starting state, settings per task, mobile behaviour, template creation and structured data—not just section inventory.
3. Catalogue Shopify Theme Store originality requirements and document feature provenance.
4. Test native Online Store 2.0 mechanisms for discoverability of intent templates and safe duplication.
5. Sample at least six real catalogues across beauty, apparel and food/lifestyle, including incomplete data and poor assets.
6. Record evidence with source, capture date, observation, inference, confidence and design implication.

## Market risk

Operability is harder to demonstrate in screenshots than visual novelty. It requires an excellent demo, concise onboarding and observable workflow advantages. “Easy to use” is not defensible language by itself; usability benchmarks and comparative task evidence must substantiate it.
